## 2. What is covered

- **KV precision and quality.** FP8/8-bit is near-lossless on reasoning (Quant Hurts Reasoning; vLLM FP8 blog) and 4-bit is near-lossless for 14B+ models; 3-bit and 2-bit hurt small models and can lengthen outputs. TriAxialKV is the one study that tests function calling (BFCL Memory) and a computer-use agent, and finds role-aware INT2/INT4 within about 1 point; UltraQuant tests SWE-bench Lite and shows noisy, model-dependent agent loss (Qwen3-235B FP8 21 vs BF16 28 out of 100).
- **Fixed prompt.** Tool reduction is established on Orin (Less-is-More, CarbonCall), with success, time and power gains at 19-46 tools; RAG-MCP and MCP-Zero for hundreds to thousands of tools; TSCG shows the exact overflow failure (28 tools do not fit 8K) and a 50% schema saving; CompressAgent gives a quality-vs-budget frontier.
- **Context management.** Observation masking, pruning and compression keep or improve success at 40-60% fewer tokens on coding and enterprise agents (Complexity Trap, ACON, Less Context Better Agents), with a counterexample on cost (Token Reduction Is Not Cost Reduction).
- **Run-time choice.** Precedents exist for weights and power (MorphServe memory-pressure layer swapping; CarbonCall on Orin), for per-token KV bits (Don't Waste Bits, ARKV under a memory budget) and for offline role-based KV bits (TriAxialKV).

## 3. What is still open for P5

1. No work I found measures **energy per successful task** for any of these levers on an agent workload; edge evidence for tool reduction reports device power or time, not task energy; KV quantization papers report throughput.
2. No work combines KV precision, tool-definition size and tool-output caps in one study on the **same agent** to compare their capacity yield per quality point (a "capacity budget" view).
3. Nothing found on **hybrid models** (sliding-window, DeltaNet) with FP8 KV in agent loops; vLLM notes FP8 gains are small on sliding-window layers.
4. **Run-time choice of these levers from live KV-pool pressure** with multiple concurrent agents on a unified-memory edge device: MorphServe (weights, datacenter) and CarbonCall (power/weights, driven by carbon and TPS) are the nearest; no KV-precision or context-budget controller for agents was found. Absence is based on searches, not exhaustive.
5. Small-model, thinking-heavy, real-time closed-loop agents (P1) are absent from all agent-quality evidence; the 7-tool case is the opposite regime from tool-retrieval papers (19-2,800 tools).
6. Whether FP8/INT4 KV actually runs fast on Orin (sm_87) and Thor is unverified; SGLang docs warn about unfused dequantization.

## 4. Novelty paragraph (option CAP)

Each lever has been studied, and the closest pieces are on the shelf: role-aware 2/4-bit KV on tool-calling benchmarks (TriAxialKV), FP8 KV validated on reasoning (vLLM, AMD), tool selection on a Jetson Orin with power numbers (Less-is-More, CarbonCall), and observation masking with halved cost (Complexity Trap). A configuration study that applies them to P1's agents and reports energy per successful task would be useful engineering and a fair measurement paper, but it is mostly configuration, and with 7 tools the tool-retrieval lever has a small ceiling. What could be new is a serving-layer controller that moves among levers (KV precision, window or tool-output cap, tool subset) as pool occupancy changes across concurrent agents, judged on task success and energy; I found no such controller, but ARKV, MorphServe and CarbonCall are close in spirit, and TriAxialKV already shows static role-based precision is nearly free in quality. The risk is that a good static setting (FP8 plus capped outputs) captures most of the gain, leaving the run-time part marginal. Honest framing: a measured design-space study first, controller second, claimed only if the static settings leave a measurable gap.
